Chapter One Introduction

The opening pages of the book, exactly as printed.

This book is the celebration of my forty-year career as a forensic consultant, specifically a financial one. When I began my niche career as a business appraiser in 1979, the word “forensic” wasn’t even used as a descriptive term for a professional service. The formal definition of the word is “relating to or denoting the application of scientific methods and techniques to the investigation of crime.” It also means “relating to courts of law.”

I began my earlier, short-lived career in public accounting after college but soon became disillusioned with its monotony and tedium. Quickly abandoning that unfulfilling path, I embarked on a law school education, only to learn that the fate of an apprentice business lawyer was just as boring as that of a staff accountant. Those experiences led me to try my hand at business brokerage, which soon evolved to business appraisal, the professional service that became the basis of my forensic consulting career.

In the beginning, I didn’t know that people only needed their businesses appraised if they were in some kind of dispute—usually a partnership split or a divorce—but I soon learned that business appraisal was litigation-related. The initial potential clients were almost all lawyers who needed business appraisers as experts in their cases to solve valuation disputes between their clients. Providing business valuations looked to be a service that I could elevate to a higher professional level rather than merely being a salesman of business opportunities. I was on my way to building a professional services firm.

One of my early business appraisals resulted in my first experience as an expert witness, and it was horrifying. I received a call from a panicked lawyer who was soon scheduled for trial in his case representing Suzie’s Ice Creamery1 against the landlord whom Suzie claimed had breached her lease in the idyllic commercial district of Coronado, California. A few hundred yards from the famous Hotel del Coronado, Suzie’s had operated successfully until her landlord abruptly canceled the sublease that granted her occupancy of a small portion of his larger retail store on prosperous Orange Avenue, the tony commercial district of the quaint beach town. Suzie’s lawyer had filed suit to recover the losses she sustained from losing her remaining three years of occupancy after finally achieving profitability and expecting to recover her early start-up losses. The justice of Suzie’s case was compelling but, unfortunately, Suzie did not have the good fortune of being well-represented.

1 Not the real business name.

About Brian

Marine. CPA. Lawyer. Expert witness.

A nonconforming teenager who finished at the bottom of his high school class, Brian enlisted in the U.S. Marine Corps and served a tour in Vietnam before going on to Georgetown University. He started in public accounting, earned his law degree at the University of San Diego, and in 1979 began appraising businesses: the work that became a forty-year career as a forensic consultant.

He founded a large financial consulting firm, later acquired by CBIZ; today he serves as Managing Director of the CBIZ Forensic Consulting Group in San Diego. Since 1997 he has taught Finance & Accounting for Lawyers, a course he designed, at the University of San Diego School of Law.
